Cookie Policy

Last updated October 7, 2026. This applies to the ParkRadar website. It goes with the Privacy Policy.

The short version

  • ParkRadar does not use cookies. In our testing, loading and using the website sets no cookies in your browser.
  • It does use your browser's local storage and similar storage. That works like a cookie jar that stays on your device. ParkRadar uses it only to keep the app working and to remember your settings.
  • No advertising, marketing or analytics cookies or trackers of our own. We do not use the storage to follow you around other websites.
  • You choose whether the map and font companies load. The first time you visit, a banner asks. Until you tap Allow, ParkRadar does not load Mapbox's maps or Google's font, so nothing from them is saved in your browser. Google Firebase, which runs live reports, is needed for ParkRadar to work and loads either way.
  • You can clear all of it at any time. Everything still works without it, but ParkRadar will forget you.

What cookies and local storage are

A cookie is a small text file that a website puts in your browser, and your browser sends it back to the site on every request. Local storage and IndexedDB are similar browser features. A site saves information in them, and the information stays on your device until it is cleared. Unlike cookies, they are not sent to the website automatically with every request.

Your choice about maps and fonts

  • Allow. The street and satellite maps and the Archivo font load. Those companies can see your IP address, and Mapbox saves the anonymous ID and map cache listed above.
  • No thanks. ParkRadar shows the parking lots on a plain background, without streets or satellite photos, and uses your browser's standard font. Nothing is loaded from Mapbox or Google Fonts. The rest of ParkRadar works the same, including reports, lots, routes and the trip planner.
  • Change your mind. Open the Me tab, then "Maps and fonts from other companies". Switching to No thanks removes what Mapbox saved and reloads the page. If you pick Streets or Satellite in the map menu without having allowed them, ParkRadar asks first.
  • Not asked yet. Until you answer, it behaves as if you said No thanks.

How long it lasts

Everything above stays in your browser until you clear it, or the browser clears it for you. ParkRadar does not set an expiry date. Your saved settings are meant to last, so you do not have to enter them again.

Your choices

  • Clear it. Delete the site's data in your browser's settings. On iPhone, in Safari that is Settings, then Safari, then Advanced, then Website Data (the steps vary by iOS version and browser). If you added ParkRadar to your Home Screen, deleting that icon also deletes its data.
  • Reset inside the app. "Reset my settings" in the Me tab clears your permit, vehicle and saved car. "Delete my data" goes further: it removes your data from our database and your anonymous account, and clears everything listed above from this browser.
  • Block it. You can set your browser to block site data. ParkRadar still opens, but it cannot remember your settings or keep you signed in anonymously, so each visit may start fresh.
  • What clearing does. You will lose your saved settings and car on this browser. Because the anonymous sign-in is stored there too, you may no longer be able to remove reports you posted earlier. See the Privacy Policy.

The iPhone app

The ParkRadar iPhone app does not use cookies either. It keeps the same kinds of information in the app's own storage on your phone, such as your anonymous sign-in (in the Keychain), your settings, and a saved copy of recent reports. Deleting the app removes that storage, though the Keychain can keep the anonymous sign-in. Mapbox's map code in the app also keeps its own anonymous ID and map cache. The Privacy Policy explains how information is handled.

Changes to this policy

If ParkRadar starts using cookies or other trackers, or changes what it stores, we will update this page and the date at the top before it happens.
