Privacy Policy

Last updated October 7, 2026. This applies to the ParkRadar iPhone app and the ParkRadar website.

The short version

  • An account is optional. ParkRadar offers to create one the first time you open it, and "Continue without an account" is always there. By default it signs you in anonymously, with no name, email or phone number. If you choose to create an account, ParkRadar keeps your email address and a password (which we cannot read) so you can log in again.
  • No ads, no selling your data, no tracking you across other apps or websites. ParkRadar has no advertising or analytics tools of its own.
  • Location is optional. ParkRadar uses it only while the app is open, and only after you allow it. On iPhone, Mapbox's map software says it can also collect location for its own analytics. See below.
  • Reports you post are public. Everyone using ParkRadar can see them. Your car, permit and settings are private.
  • You can delete your data from inside the app, with the "Delete my data" button in the Me tab. Questions or requests: parkradar@parkradarapp.com.

What ParkRadar collects, at a glance

Each item says what it is, why we collect it, who processes it, how long we keep it and how you delete it. Email addresses and notification tokens are on the list because people ask about them. ParkRadar collects an email address only if you create an account, and it does not collect notification tokens.

Anonymous ID

Collected

Email address and password

Only if you tap Create account: so you can log in again and keep your settings and saved car if you change phones or browsers, and so we can send a password reset when you ask. How to delete it
Me tab, then Delete my data. It removes your account and your email address, after you enter your password to confirm.

Your location (where you are right now)

Only if you allow it, and we do not store it
Why
To show you on the map, work out how far you are from your car, draw a walking route, and place a pin where you are.
Processed by
Your own device. On iPhone, Mapbox's map software says it can also collect location for its own analytics (see "What Mapbox's iPhone software says it collects" below). On the website your position stays in your browser. The one exception: if you tap Estimate from my location in the trip planner or class schedule, your position and the position of a campus lot are sent to Mapbox's directions service to get a driving time. That happens only when you tap, on the website only if you have allowed Mapbox, and ParkRadar does not keep your position afterward.
How long we keep it
We do not keep it. It is used only while the app is open.
How to delete it
There is nothing stored to delete. You can switch location off for ParkRadar in iPhone Settings, or in your browser's site settings.

Parked-car location

Collected when you save your car
Why
To show where you parked, how far away it is, a route back, enforcement alerts near your car, and parking-time reminders. It is saved as a spot on the campus map, the nearest lot and the time, not as a trail of coordinates. When you save it from your location ("I'm parked here"), the spot is your exact position at that moment, and we also keep how close the phone said the reading was, in meters.
Processed by
Google Firebase, in your private settings that only your anonymous account can read. A copy is also kept on your device.
How long we keep it
Until you remove the car, save a new spot, reset your settings or delete your data.
How to delete it
Open your saved car and tap the button to remove it, or use Reset my settings or Delete my data in the Me tab.

A reminder stays on your phone until it goes off or you cancel it.

Reports and "how full" updates

Collected when you post
What
The spot you chose on the campus map, the type (enforcement or closure), the time, anything you add (vehicle type, plate or unit number, a note, how long a closure lasts), and your anonymous ID. For a "how full" update: the lot, the level, the time and your anonymous ID.
Why
So everyone using ParkRadar can see where enforcement and closures are and how full lots are. "How full" updates are also kept as history so predictions can learn. These are public.

Google Firebase.

How to delete it
Open a report you posted and remove it, or use Delete my data in the Me tab, which removes your reports, your "how full" updates and their history.

Which services process it. Only these handle your information: Google Firebase (sign-in and the database), Mapbox (the street and satellite maps, and driving times when you ask for one, and on the website only if you allow them), and GitHub (which hosts the website's pages). If you allow the website's font, Google Fonts also sees your IP address. They are described in "Who can see it" below.

In this policy, "we" means Park Radar LLC, the company that builds and runs ParkRadar. How to reach us is on the About page.

What ParkRadar collects

An anonymous ID, and an optional account

You do not need an account. If you create one, you give an email address and choose a password. Firebase Authentication keeps them, and the password only in a scrambled form we cannot read. Creating an account upgrades the anonymous ID below, so what you already posted and saved stays with you. Logging in on another phone or browser switches it to your account's ID, and logging out goes back to a new anonymous ID. Reports you posted before you logged in stay under the older ID. We do not use your email for anything except your account and the password reset you ask for.

The first time you open ParkRadar, it asks Google's Firebase Authentication for an anonymous account. That gives your phone or browser a random ID, with no name or email attached. We use it to mark which reports are yours (so only you can remove them), to keep your private settings, and to count each person's "still there" and "it's gone" taps once. The ID is stored on your device: in the iPhone Keychain, or in your browser's storage. The website does not use cookies. The Cookie Policy lists everything it stores in your browser, and explains the choice the website gives you about maps and fonts from other companies.

Reports you post

When you report enforcement or a road closure, ParkRadar stores the spot you chose on the campus map, the nearest lot or landmark, the time, and your anonymous ID. For enforcement you may also add the type of vehicle, a plate or unit number, and a note. For a closure you add a short description and how long it lasts. Tapping "Still there" or "It's gone" on someone's report adds your anonymous ID and the time to that report.

All of this is visible to everyone using ParkRadar. The plate or unit number field is meant for enforcement vehicles. Please do not enter personal information about private individuals in any report.

"How full is it?" reports

When you tap how full a lot is, ParkRadar stores the lot, the level, the time, and your anonymous ID. Everyone can see the latest report for each lot. Every report is also added to a history, which ParkRadar uses to predict how busy lots will be at different times.

Your private settings

These are kept in a private record that only your anonymous account can read or change:

  • your permit type, and your residential area if you have one;
  • a description of your vehicle, if you typed one (for example "Toyota Tacoma, gray");
  • your alert distance, and whether pop-up alerts are on;
  • whether you joined the ParkRadar+ waitlist. This is only a yes or no. We do not collect an email address for it;
  • your saved car: its spot on the campus map, the lot, when you parked, how accurate your location reading was (if you saved it from your location), and any parking-time reminder.

Your location

ParkRadar uses your location only while it is open, and only if you allow it. On iPhone it asks for "While Using the App" permission the first time you tap something that needs it: the locate button, saving your car at your current location, or "Here" when reporting. On the website your browser asks. ParkRadar uses your location to show where you are on the map, measure how far you are from your car, draw a walking route back to it, and place a pin.

ParkRadar does not track you in the background and does not keep a location history. Your live position stays on your device. A location is stored only when you choose to use it for something that is saved: saving where you parked (kept in your private settings) or placing a report (public). Even then it is saved as a spot on the campus map, not as a stream of coordinates.

Trip planner and reminders

The class building, class time, days and drive time you enter in "Plan my trip" stay on your device. If you use Estimate from my location to fill in the drive time, your current position and a campus lot go to Mapbox once to get the answer, and only the number of minutes is kept. Your choice about the "how full was it?" question that appears after you save your car in a lot, and whether the app is light, dark or follows your device, also stay on your device, and nothing is sent unless you tap one of the answers. "Remind me to leave" and parking-time reminders are scheduled on your phone as local notifications. The class schedule in ParkRadar+ (not for sale yet) would work the same way: your classes (name, building, time, days, drive time and where you want to park) are kept only on your phone, and its reminders are local notifications too. ParkRadar does not send push notifications from a server.

What Mapbox's iPhone software says it collects

The iPhone app uses Mapbox's map software. Mapbox's own privacy declaration for that software says it can collect precise location, approximate location and an anonymous identifier, for app functionality and analytics. Mapbox says this is not linked to your identity and is not used to track you. ParkRadar does not receive it and cannot see it. It only applies if you have allowed ParkRadar to use your location. See the Mapbox telemetry page and the Mapbox privacy policy.

What your device sends automatically

Like any app or website, ParkRadar connects to other services to work, and they see your IP address and basic technical information about your device or browser. They are listed below.

How ParkRadar uses information

  • to show enforcement reports, road closures and lot availability to everyone;
  • to estimate how full a lot will be and when you should leave;
  • to remember your permit, car and settings, and to alert you about reports near your car or in your zones while the app is open;
  • to keep the service working, fix problems, and limit spam and abuse.

We do not use it for advertising, we do not build profiles of people, and we do not sell or rent it.

Who can see it

Other ParkRadar users see the reports and "how full" updates you post, along with the time. They do not see your name (we do not have it), your permit, your vehicle description or your saved car.

Services that run ParkRadar. They process data for us, under their own privacy policies:

ServiceWhat it does for ParkRadar
Google Firebase (Google privacy policy)Anonymous sign-in, optional email accounts and password resets, and the database that stores reports, lot updates, history and private settings. On the website, Google also serves the Firebase code, and the Archivo font if you allow it.

How long it is kept

  • Enforcement reports stop showing 15 minutes after the last sighting, and closures stop showing when they end. The next time you post a report, ParkRadar removes your own reports that are more than about 12 hours old. Other people's expired reports may stay in the database, but they are hidden.
  • "How full" history is kept so predictions can learn. ParkRadar uses roughly the last eight weeks, but older entries can remain stored until you delete them with "Delete my data" or we remove them.
  • A block list. If we block someone for breaking the Terms of Service, we keep the anonymous ID or device identifier we blocked on a block list for as long as needed to stop them coming back.
  • Your private settings and saved car are kept until you reset them, remove the car, or ask us to delete them.
  • On your device, data stays until you reset, clear it, or delete the app.

Your choices

  • Location. Change it any time in iPhone Settings, Privacy & Security, Location Services, ParkRadar (or in your browser's site settings). ParkRadar still works without it: you can tap the map instead.
  • Notifications. Turn them off in iPhone Settings, Notifications, ParkRadar. Today, reminders are only set when you tap a reminder button. If ParkRadar+ launches with its class schedule, turning on its Daily reminders switch will also set one reminder for each class day on your phone, and turning the switch off removes them.
  • Reset my settings. In the Me tab, this clears your permit, vehicle description, saved car and class schedule.
  • Delete my data. Also in the Me tab. It removes your reports, your "how full" updates and their history, your private settings and saved car, and your anonymous account (or, if you made one, your account and email address, after you enter your password), and then clears what ParkRadar saved on your device. It cannot be undone. If a delete fails, nothing is cleared and you can try again.
  • Remove one report. Open a report you posted and tap the button to remove it.
  • Delete the app or clear the website's data to remove what is stored on your device. On iPhone, the anonymous sign-in can stay in the Keychain after you delete the app, so reinstalling may bring back the same anonymous account and settings.
  • Ask us. ParkRadar does not know who you are, so we cannot look you up by name. If you want something removed that you cannot remove yourself, email us with what it was and roughly when you posted it, and we will do what we can. A "still there" or "it's gone" tap on someone else's report is stored only with an anonymous ID and goes away with that report. It cannot be removed on its own.

If you live somewhere with privacy laws that give you extra rights, such as access or deletion, contact us and we will honor them as far as we can.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections. The database has rules that limit what each person can read and change, and your private settings can be read only by your own anonymous account. No system is perfectly secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Children

ParkRadar is for people who drive to campus and is not directed at children under 15. The apps ask you to confirm that you are 15 or older before you start, and the Terms of Service require it. ParkRadar does not ask for a name or birth date, and asks for an email address only if you choose to create an account. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 15. If you think a child has used ParkRadar, contact us and we will delete what we can find, or the child or a parent can use the "Delete my data" button.
